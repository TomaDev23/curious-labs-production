## Q1 · The three problems

**Answer.** The most valuable thing to come out of the discussion was the *concept* of how to
use the technology. The owner's usage is a **zoomed-out operator** view, while most people are
still using AI to change small things and work in small steps. He can explain prompt design,
the limitations of AI, where to trust it and where not to, and how to leverage it without it
failing you — through the **harness** concept, ongoing auditing, and distributing work across a
team of agents. This is advanced knowledge that does not touch the question of how to code.

Two distinct audiences: people who already code or have teams building products and want to use
AI better; and people with no code involvement at all who want to add AI to a business —
factories, restaurants, hotels — for personal assistance, management, or creation. Both need
the same concepts: harness management, awareness of limitations.

Reduced to three problems, in his words:

1. Turn AI **from brute force and an unexpected miracle into a trusted team member**. Put the
   harness around the wild capabilities.
2. Give tools to teach, educate, and create tools so people can use AI **without it collapsing
   under its own weight** as things become complicated again.
3. Help people actually achieve their ideas, by giving them the knowledge and a harness to work
   within, so they achieve **more reliably**.

**Publishable.** All three problem statements, the two-audience split, the operator-view framing,
and the harness/auditing/limits vocabulary as *topics*.

**Reserved.** Nothing.

## Q10 · Training, format, and cost

**Answer.** All the training options are possible. At minimum, a **report and one-on-one
sessions** with the relevant team members or the business owner. The training is then tailored
to the relevant entity in the company, to the length of the work, and to the type of support
required. It can be ongoing support, or weekly meetings for a period. **No fixed solution and
no fixed cost.**

*"I'm not offering AI training as a general thing. I am offering a tailored solution for a
business that focuses on understanding someone's needs and giving him answers and solutions
within what he needs."* It is about talking with whoever matters in the company — a CEO or
managers — and developing a plan with them that answers their needs and helps them use the
technology.

**Publishable.** The report + one-to-one sessions baseline; tailoring to entity, length and
support; ongoing support and weekly meetings as possibilities; no fixed cost.

**Reserved — tone.** *"…not just coming and dropping what I think about AI and giving a $1,000
receipt."* Right instinct, wrong register for the page, and it introduces a price. Paraphrase as
the positive: the plan answers their needs. Do not publish a figure.

## Q13 · From meeting to delivery

**Answer.** After a meeting, the client gets an **immediate email** confirming and generally

that deliverable, then receive a **contract and payment arrangement**. Once that is sorted he
begins preparing the actual full materials and the time frame to deliver. Worth showing as a
flow. *"It's quite obvious, so we don't need to make it silly obvious."*

**Publishable.** The four-step flow; the three distinct documents (summary → proposal/quote →
paid deliverable); that preparation begins after the arrangements are sorted.

**Reserved.** "A few days" is his intention, not a contractual deadline — keep it soft. No fixed
price, deposit percentage, or automated scheduling.
