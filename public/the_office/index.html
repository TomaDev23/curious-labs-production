THE OFFICE

      // orchestration floor · v0.3 · vertical authority spine + a side service/peer band
    

    
A real office floor for your agents. Geography is the rulebook. An office is a capability, not a swarm: one manager runs its responsibilities as sub-agents in a single thread — only Audit Rooms are always a fresh adversarial agent, and Intern Pools are script-scaled mechanical crews. Two tiers split by the Ledger. Tier A carries the vertical authority spine plus a non-dispatching service/peer band (Agent Resources, Mapping, Creative/Visual, DevOps) that serves every floor. Each work floor has specialist offices (the homes, where agents have desks) plus co-working spaces spawned one-per-campaign — Meeting Rooms in R&D, Workshops (the Build Floor in action) in the Factory, and Command Co-Working Rooms above the barrier for campaign management — where the specialists gather to work, so you read the room, not a racing list of agents. ⚑ Method charter — the framing

            
Not the cheapest way to work — but blind agent swarms are expensive and less productive. We spread load across many scope-bounded agents (some ultracode campaign agents, some periodic light ones) so none holds too much context, coordinate by the company hierarchy, and talk through the Ledger. Cost · usage · context-awareness is existential — mechanical pruning keeps it lean.

Briefing

      
Two tiers, one barrier

      
// command above · work below

      

        
Tier A — Command is a vertical authority spine. You sit at the apex with two comms lanes, and the Navigator — the Captain Loop works alongside you. Every project opens with an operator interview (a few ping-pong rounds that set scope, environment & risk). Under you, the GM plans who does what; the Ledger is now a hierarchy — one cheap appender per ledger, managers read the tail. Operational managers sit under the GM: Task, Shadow, and the Mapping Office (the records umbrella — Campaign Mapper · Archive · Librarian, dedicated agents). Beside them a service / peer band of non-dispatching offices serves every floor: Agent Resources (designs the wave, hands it off), Creative/Visual (never send words — always visualize), and DevOps — the only door to production: agents commit, but nothing pushes without your logged OK. Isolation is a planning principle, not an office.

        
The Ledger is the barrier. Above it, command. Below it, work.

        
Tier B — Work is two isolated departments. Each holds specialist offices (the homes) and co-working spaces (where the work happens). An office is a capability: one manager runs its responsibilities as sub-agents in one thread (the Studio folds Math/Planner/Sim; the Build Floor folds the field offices). Only the Audit Rooms are always a fresh, separate adversarial agent; the Intern Pools are script-scaled mechanical crews that serve both offices.

        
Two axes. Offices answer what kind of work (fixed homes, where agents keep desks). Co-working spaces answer which campaign — one room per campaign, numbered and spawnable, where the specialists come in from their desks to work. Meeting Rooms on the R&D floor (plan & review); Workshops on the Factory floor — the Build Floor is its workshops, the production lines where building actually happens. So a busy floor reads as a handful of rooms — you check the room, never a racing list of agents.

        
Tap any office or co-working space to inspect it and the law it enforces.
